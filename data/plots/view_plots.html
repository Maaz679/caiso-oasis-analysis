Locational Marginal Price (LMP)

Chapter 5: LMP Decomposition

What This Shows

LMP represents the cost of delivering one additional MW of electricity at a specific location. This visualization breaks it down into three key components:

  • Energy Component (Green): Base cost of generating electricity (approximately 81%)
  • Congestion Component (Orange): Cost due to transmission constraints (approximately 15%)
  • Loss Component (Red): Cost of electrical losses during transmission (approximately negative 3%)

Key Insight: Notice the price spike in morning hours. This is when demand rises but cheap solar has not ramped up yet. Negative prices around 3 PM indicate oversupply from solar generation.

Trading Hub Comparison

Regional Price Differences

What This Shows

Compares electricity prices across California's three major trading hubs:

  • NP15 (Northern CA): Import-constrained region, often higher prices
  • SP15 (Southern CA): Major load center near LA
  • ZP26 (San Diego): Southern tip, can experience extreme price swings

Price Spread Analysis (Bottom Panel): Shows each hub's deviation from system average. NP15 trading $10-40/MWh above average indicates transmission congestion from north to south.

3

Generation Mix & Duck Curve

Chapter 3: Economic Dispatch

What This Shows

California's generation portfolio stacked by fuel type, plus battery storage activity:

  • Solar (Yellow): Dominates midday, creating the famous "duck curve"
  • Baseload (Blue/Teal): Nuclear and hydro provide constant generation
  • Flexible (Red/Green): Natural gas and wind adjust to demand
  • Batteries (Purple/Pink): Charge during solar surplus, discharge during peak

Duck Curve: The bottom chart shows batteries charging approximately 7,000 MW during midday solar oversupply and discharging approximately 8,000 MW during evening peak when solar drops off.

Average Generation Portfolio

Merit Order Stack

What This Shows

California's average generation mix over the analysis period (23,049 MW total):

  • Solar: 30.3% - Leading renewable source
  • Imports: 19.3% - Electricity from neighboring states
  • Large Hydro: 13.3% - Major renewable baseload
  • Natural Gas: 10.9% - Flexible generation for peaks
  • Wind: 10.1% - Growing renewable source
  • Nuclear: 10.1% - Clean baseload (Diablo Canyon)

California's Clean Energy: Over 60% of generation is carbon-free (renewables + nuclear + large hydro).

System Load Profile

Demand Analysis

What This Shows

Total electricity demand across California's grid with key statistics:

  • Minimum Load: Approximately 18,000 MW (overnight hours)
  • Maximum Load: Approximately 27,000 MW (evening peak)
  • Average Load: Approximately 22,000 MW
  • Peak/Min Ratio: 1.47x (moderate daily variation)

Load Pattern: Classic dual-peak pattern with morning ramp-up (7-9 AM) and evening peak (5-8 PM) as people return home and solar generation declines.
