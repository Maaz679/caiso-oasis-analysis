$/MWh · RTM
Fetching LMP data from CAISO OASIS…

The LMP is the price CAISO sets every five minutes for one additional MWh at a specific grid location. It is what it actually costs the system to serve one more unit of demand right now.

Energy is the fuel cost of the marginal generator system-wide. Congestion reflects transmission constraints — it goes negative when a constraint works in your favor. Loss accounts for resistive heat losses over distance. GHG adds California's cap-and-trade carbon price when the market is active.

Negative total LMPs indicate a period of solar curtailment: more generation than the grid can absorb, forcing prices below zero to incentivize consumption or storage charging.

NP15 covers the PG&E territory (San Jose, San Francisco, Sacramento). Prices are lower when Sierra Nevada hydro and Northwest imports are available. SP15 is SCE's southern California territory — high summer cooling load, close to Mojave desert solar. ZP26 is SDG&E's San Diego zone, smaller and electrically isolated, often the most expensive due to limited import capacity.

The spread chart shows each hub relative to the three-hub average. A persistent positive spread signals a local supply shortage or congested import path. Path 26 (NP15↔SP15) and the Southwest Powerlink (into ZP26) are the two most frequently congested interfaces in California.

California Generation Mix

The Duck Curve is visible on any day with significant solar: output climbs steeply through the morning, peaks around noon, then drops sharply after 3–4 PM. As solar falls, residential demand rises — the grid must ramp Natural Gas peakers by thousands of megawatts within an hour. That fast ramp is the core operational challenge of a high-solar grid.

The battery panel below the generation stack shows charging (negative bars, absorbing surplus solar) and discharging (positive bars, releasing energy into the evening peak). California now has several GW of grid-scale BESS capacity that directly flattens the duck curve.

System Load Profile
Statewide electricity demand · US Pacific
MW · Actual
Fetching load data…

System load is total electricity consumed across California, measured in MW at 5-minute intervals from CAISO's real-time metering — not a forecast.

Demand bottoms between 2–5 AM and peaks in the early evening (5–9 PM) when residential use combines with remaining commercial load. Summer heat waves can push the all-time peak above 52,000 MW. CAISO balances generation against this curve continuously, every five minutes, maintaining frequency within 59.95–60.05 Hz.

Battery Storage Dispatch
Charge / discharge activity and estimated state of charge · US Pacific
MW · BESS
Fetching battery data…

California now operates several GW of grid-scale lithium-ion BESS. The dispatch pattern is highly predictable: charge during peak solar hours (10 AM–3 PM, when LMP goes negative) and discharge into the evening ramp (5–9 PM, when LMP peaks).

The state of charge estimate is derived by integrating the power signal over time — a proxy, not metered data, since CAISO doesn't publish real-time SOC. It shows the relative charge cycle and confirms whether the fleet has capacity available for the evening peak.

About This Dashboard
CAISO OASIS Market Analysis
A live electricity market intelligence dashboard built to explore wholesale power market dynamics and apply economic models from power systems theory to real grid data.

What this demonstrates

  • Real-time API ingestion — CAISO OASIS 5-minute interval data via gridstatus
  • Electricity market analysis — LMP decomposition, merit order clearing, hub spreads
  • Power systems visualization — duck curve, dispatch modeling, generation stack
  • Grid operations literacy — frequency regulation, curtailment, BESS dispatch cycles
  • Economic dispatch modeling — lambda iteration, quadratic cost curves (Kirschen & Strbac)

Data sources

  • CAISO OASIS API — PRC_INTVL_LMP, fuel mix, system load at 5-min resolution
  • Markets covered — Real-Time Market (RTM) and Day-Ahead Market (DAM)
  • Hubs — NP15 (PG&E), SP15 (SCE), ZP26 (SDG&E)
  • Data cached for 5 minutes per load; every refresh reflects current market state
